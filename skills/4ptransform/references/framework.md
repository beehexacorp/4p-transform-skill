# 4P Transform

> 4P Transform is a thinking framework that helps people understand change before they act, through four connected elements: Problem (Why), Product (What), People (Who) and Protocol (How). Vision: Help enterprises and individuals streamline the decision-making process with confidence.

Current version: 4P Transform v1.0, live since 2026-11-20. Author: Phan Vu Giap (https://phanvugiap.com/). The name is "4P Transform"; Product, Problem, People and Protocol are "the four Ps". Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/).

## The core question

Which specific Product empowers People to overcome a critical Problem through a clear Protocol?

## The four Ps

### Problem (Why)

The critical challenge that makes the change worth doing.

- Internal problems start inside the enterprise, in its own processes, systems and teams.
- External problems come from outside, from customers, partners and the wider market.
- Ask “why?” five times to get past the symptom to the root cause.

### Product (What)

What the enterprise builds, buys or deploys to create value.

- A product can serve the enterprise itself, its customers, or both: Internal teams, Existing customers, New customers.
- The enterprise can build it in house or buy it: Own it outright, Subscribe to it.
- Bought as a subscription, a product arrives as a service, so the line between the two is blurring.

### People (Who)

Everyone who uses the product or lives with the problem.

- Internal people work inside the enterprise: staff, teams and leaders.
- External people work outside it, yet the change reaches them too: Customers, Partners, Suppliers, The wider supply chain.

### Protocol (How)

How People, Problem and Product interact with one another.

- How people work with one another, the interaction everything else depends on: Inside teams, Across departments, With customers and partners.
- How people use the product.
- How people experience and handle the problem.
- How the product solves the problem.
- One situation often touches several Ps. Look at it through each question in turn instead of forcing it into one box.

Ways to get a Product: build in house (the enterprise develops the product itself and owns it); buy outright (the enterprise pays once and keeps the product for good); subscribe (the enterprise pays to use the product for as long as it needs it). Owned, subscribed or paid per use, it is still the Product.

## Principles

- Protocol is the centre: People, Problem and Product never stand alone: they always interact through protocols. Change can start from any P, but no P changes in isolation.
- Simplicity is the compass: Every change must be easy to understand and easy to apply.

Non-goals: 4P Transform is not a complete framework for digital transformation; not a method for choosing software or vendors; not a sales tool for any product; not a replacement for expert advice in law, finance or a specific industry; not a method for running or measuring transformation projects. It is a way to think a change through.

## How to apply

Start with any P and consider the other three. Protocol connects them; these prompts have no fixed order.

- Name the Problem: Say whether it starts inside the enterprise or outside it, then ask “why?” five times to reach the root cause.
- Describe the Product: Describe the product involved in the change, who it serves and the problem it addresses.
- List the People: Name everyone who lives with the problem or will live with the change, inside the enterprise and outside it.
- Describe the Protocol: Describe how people work together, how they use the product and how they handle the problem. Consider how these connections change.
- Answer the core question: Fill in one sentence with all four Ps. A part you can’t fill in is a question you still need to answer.

The one-page 4P Transform canvas (https://4ptransform.org/canvas/) puts Protocol in the centre; any P can be the starting point. Prompts per box:

- Protocol (How): How do people work with one another today? / How do they use the product, and how do they handle the problem? / What should change in how they work together?
- Problem (Why): Internal or external? / Why? Why? Why? Why? Why? The root cause:
- People (Who): Inside: staff, teams, leaders / Outside: customers, partners, suppliers
- Product (What): Build, buy outright or subscribe? / Who is it for: internal teams, existing or new customers?

## Kinds of transformation

Transformation does not have to start big; a change to any one of the four Ps moves the other three.

- Launch a new product.
- Reach a new customer segment.
- Change how people communicate with one another.
- Automate the manual status updates that pass between internal tools.
- Standardize UI components so navigation and onboarding get simpler.
- Add small integration points between modules you already run.

## Examples (27)

- Marketing & Sales: Product: HubSpot CRM. People: Marketing and sales teams. Problem: Finding more customers and tracking lead conversion. Protocol: Improve how marketing attracts customers and automatically track potential customers.
- Retail: Product: Loyalty app. People: Sales team. Problem: Declining customer retention rate. Protocol: Automated points accumulation and offer notifications via SMS/Zalo.
- Manufacturing: Product: IoT equipment-monitoring sensors. People: Operations engineers. Problem: Sudden machinery downtime that causes cost waste. Protocol: Use live equipment data to predict when maintenance is needed.
- Healthcare: Product: Telehealth portal. People: Doctors and patients. Problem: Overcrowding at in-person clinics and long waiting times. Protocol: Book appointments and hold consultations online using consistent steps.
- Agriculture: Product: Smart automated irrigation system. People: Farmers. Problem: Water waste and high labor costs during dry seasons. Protocol: Adjusting water volume based on real-time soil moisture data.
- Education: Product: Learning management system (LMS). People: Faculty and students. Problem: Tracking learning progress and assigning personalized homework. Protocol: Automatically handle assignment submissions and grading, with immediate feedback.
- Logistics: Product: Platform for planning efficient routes and stops. People: Drivers and dispatchers. Problem: Delivery delays and high fuel consumption. Protocol: Automated route updates based on real-time traffic.
- Hospitality: Product: Kiosk & mobile check-in. People: Front desk staff and guests. Problem: Lobby congestion during peak hours. Protocol: Automatically check identities and issue digital room keys.
- Energy: Product: Advanced metering infrastructure (AMI). People: Technical staff. Problem: Manual meter reading errors and delayed leakage detection. Protocol: Continuously send electricity-use readings and automatically flag power losses.
- Real Estate: Product: VR tour. People: Brokers and homebuyers. Problem: The time buyers spend having to visit properties in person. Protocol: Tour properties virtually and pay deposits online using consistent steps.
- Entertainment: Product: QR code ticketing and verification system. People: Operations staff and moviegoers. Problem: The inconvenience of long ticket lines. Protocol: Online seat selection and instant QR code scanning.
- Finance: Product: eKYC. People: Tellers and customers. Problem: Long wait times and complex paperwork when opening accounts. Protocol: Automatically check faces and identity documents to verify identities.
- Transportation: Product: Real-time electronic schedule boards. People: Passengers and station management. Problem: Congestion and lack of trip information. Protocol: Use GPS location data to update departure times instantly.
- Construction: Product: Building information modeling (BIM). People: Architects and site engineers. Problem: Blueprint conflicts and material waste. Protocol: Find conflicting parts in 3D building models and track construction progress in one place.
- Ride-hailing (Uber): Product: Ride-hailing app. People: Passengers and drivers. Problem: Difficulty catching rides, with hassles and non-transparent pricing. Protocol: Automatically assign rides, locate drivers and passengers with GPS, and take cashless payments.
- Generative AI: Product: Generative AI assistant. People: Content creators and customer support teams. Problem: Time-consuming drafting of documents, emails and marketing content. Protocol: Automatically draft content, tailor messages and combine information as it arrives.
- Accounting & Finance: Product: Automated invoice processing system. People: Finance team and vendors. Problem: Delays and errors from manual invoice data entry and approval bottlenecks. Protocol: Automatically read invoice text, match invoices with purchase orders and delivery records, and handle approvals digitally.
- Sales Leadership Transition: Product: Knowledge management and CRM sales pipeline platform. People: Sales team and executive leadership. Problem: Business disruption, lost deal context and customer relationship risk when a sales leader quits. Protocol: Track deals in one place, automatically pass on deal information and follow shared sales guidance.
- Removing Hierarchy in Communication: Product: Slack / Microsoft Teams. People: Employees across all levels and executive leadership. Problem: Slow, rigid, top-down communication bottlenecks. Protocol: Message one another directly, use channels across teams and discuss issues openly.
- Adding Sales Channel: Product: Social commerce module. People: Sales team and online shoppers. Problem: Revenue stagnation from relying on a single sales channel. Protocol: Buy directly on social media and automatically keep order details in sync.
- E-commerce Expansion: Product: Online storefront & marketplace connector. People: Sales and fulfillment teams. Problem: Limited reach and revenue caps from physical stores. Protocol: Automatically keep stock levels consistent across sales channels, manage orders digitally and process payments together.
- Switching from MS 365 to Google Workspace: Product: Google Workspace. People: Employees and IT teams. Problem: High software licence costs, the work of maintaining older desktop software and difficulty working together in real time. Protocol: Edit shared documents online, manage cloud services in one place and communicate through connected web tools.
- AI Customer Support: Product: AI customer support agent. People: Support agents and customer service managers. Problem: High ticket volumes, long response delays and 24/7 coverage gaps. Protocol: Automatically identify what customers need, answer common questions and pass conversations to human support agents.
- AI Predictive Analytics in Supply Chain: Product: Predictive supply chain platform. People: Inventory managers and procurement teams. Problem: Stockouts, excess inventory costs and inaccurate manual forecasting. Protocol: Use AI to analyze past trends and current market changes, and automatically trigger reorders.
- AI Code Generation & Refactoring: Product: AI coding assistant. People: Software engineers and QA testers. Problem: Slow software development, extra work caused by past coding shortcuts and repeatedly writing standard code. Protocol: Suggest code as it is written, automatically create tests for individual code parts and review code with AI.
- Beverages / Everyday Consumer Goods (Coca-Cola Product Launch): Product: New beverage product line. People: Brand marketing team and retail partners. Problem: Shifting consumer preferences and market competition risks. Protocol: Run promotions automatically, coordinate distribution and track sales as they happen.
- EU Regulatory Compliance & Competitive Strategy: Product: EU regulatory compliance platform. People: Compliance officers and strategy executives. Problem: Navigating complex EU digital regulations while staying competitive against US rivals. Protocol: Automatically record actions for review, continuously monitor policies and manage data under shared rules.
